The Five-Day Workweek and Spatial Labor Supply: Evidence from South Korea
Working Paper
Daehee Kim (Solo Author)

This paper studies whether working-time regulation reshapes the spatial allocation of labor by reducing commuting intensity. I exploit the staggered introduction of the Five-Day Workweek in South Korea, which lowered commuting frequency from six to five workdays per week. Using individual-level panel data and an intention-to-treat exposure measure based on statutory eligibility, I show that short-tenure Seoul workers become more likely to reside in Gyeonggi after legal eligibility begins. These responses are heterogeneous across workers and concentrated on the Seoul–Gyeonggi residential margin.

Presented at: KER International Conference (Jeju, June 2026); The 2nd Sogang Global Symposuim on the Frontiers of Economics (Seoul, August 2026).

Historical Instruments and Exclusion Restrictions: A Replication Study of Roche(2020)
Working Paper (Submitted)
Daehee Kim, Sungwon Lee, Jiho Yeom

This paper conducts narrow and wide replications of Roche (2020), which uses historical instruments to estimate causal effects, and evaluates the validity of the exclusion restrictions for the instruments. We rely on the framework of Masten and Poirier (2021) and estimate the falsification frontier and falsification adaptive set. We find that the baseline model, which imposes exact exclusion on both instruments, is falsified, and that the original sign conclusion of Roche (2020) is not preserved over all minimally non-falsified relaxations of the exclusion restrictions. However, it is maintained over a large portion of the relaxations. The recovery of the sign conclusion depends on which instrument is maintained as valid. In our preferred specification, the estimated effect is positive and similar in magnitude to that in Roche (2020). In doing so, we illustrate how sensitivity analysis of the exclusion restriction can be conducted, including how the robustness of a conclusion depends on which instrument is maintained as valid.
